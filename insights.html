Practical engineering insights

Concise perspectives drawn from experience in design, assessment, rehabilitation, research, and project delivery. These are general observations, not project-specific advice.

Bridge rehabilitation should begin with uncertainty management

A good rehabilitation strategy links inspection findings, deterioration mechanisms, structural analysis, constructability, risk, and lifecycle cost. The objective is not simply to repair visible defects, but to preserve safe and reliable performance over the intended service life.

Diagnostic load testing should answer a defined engineering question

Instrumentation and loading should be selected to verify specific uncertainties such as stiffness, distribution, deflection, strain response, or elastic behaviour. A load test is most valuable when its acceptance criteria and decision pathways are established before testing.

Tower assessments depend on configuration, appurtenances, and load path

For communication towers, guyed masts, monopoles, and rooftop structures, assessment must account for current antenna loading, wind exposure, connections, foundations, corrosion, modifications, and the governing edition of the applicable standard.

Performance-based analysis must remain connected to constructability

Nonlinear methods can reveal structural response that simplified checks cannot. Their value depends on realistic modelling assumptions, appropriate acceptance criteria, transparent interpretation, and a strengthening solution that can be built and inspected.
